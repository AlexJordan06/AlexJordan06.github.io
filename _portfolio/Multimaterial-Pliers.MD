# Description

* Overall Project
  * This project was focused on making a pair of pliers that would not only work with large objects, but be able to successfully pick up a resistor with relative ease off of a surface to demonstrate its proficiency. The interior of the pliers was printed with TPU-95A, which is far more flexible than the PLA used to print the harder handles and plier piece. The flexibility of the middle piece, when paired with the slicer settings being modified to only have infill for that area of the print for added flexibility, allow the pliers to move as a unit when pinched from the sides of the handle and work effectively. 

* Design Process
  * I was fortunate enough to only need one printed iteration which worked perfectly as intended. This was due to the fact that I utilized Fusion 360 CAD design, and made sure that the pliers were exactly as I wanted them before I printed even a prototype. This allowed for much faster creation of the final project, and did not waste any material as well. The jaw length is almost 20 mm, and the width between them in their resting state is right around 10 mm. When pinched, the pliers favor coming to a point at the end rather than having uniform force across the face of the contact area.

* Print in Place Parts
  * There are many different ways that print in place parts have been made using 3D printing, and many different applications for those parts. For starters, print-in-place purely means that it is something printed that has complex features that allow it to do things such as move or interlock with other segments of the print. This can be done both with one material and with multiple different types. An example of a one material print-in-place part is a fairly common one, being toys and fidgets. These are able to be made fairly easily, and can create chains that link the pieces together as long as support material is removed in the gaps. (Enter link here for one material example). For multimaterial print-in-place parts, they can be used either for different colors in the design, or adding to a functional element such as this (Enter gear multimaterial print here) example, where the model uses a harder filament for the body of the gear but a flexible one on the contact surfaces that allow it to gain less wear and tear over time. 
  
  * Materials that work best for print in place parts are ones that have similar chemical compositions and surface energy. The surface energy is measured by the contact angle that a drop of liquid forms with the material, and if that angle is similar between two materials their surface energy is fairly similar.
